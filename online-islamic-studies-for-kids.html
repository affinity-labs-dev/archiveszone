What “safe for kids” means here

Any page can claim it, so here are the specifics: what the app does, and what it deliberately doesn’t.

  • ✓
    The lesson ends

    One story, one question, done. There is no infinite feed, no autoplay into the next thing, and no algorithm deciding what your child sees after that. The screen time has a natural stopping point built into it.

  • ✓
    Nothing to buy inside your child’s lesson

    Subscriptions are handled on the web by a parent, not through a checkout in the middle of a story. Your child is never the one being sold to.

  • ✓
    Sourced history, not myth

    Every story is built from historical accounts. Where scholars disagree, the story says so instead of picking the most dramatic version and presenting it as settled fact.

  • ✓
    Calm language, no graphic detail

    Battles, loss and hardship are part of history, so they aren’t skipped. They are written plainly and without the gore, in the same measured tone as everything else.

  • ✓
    The account belongs to you

    Archives is designed for a parent to set up and a child to learn on. If your child is under 13, the account should be yours. Our privacy policy has the full detail, and it takes two minutes to read before you hand over any app.

Frequently asked questions

What are online Islamic studies for kids?

Lessons a child can do at home on a phone or tablet, rather than only in a classroom. Archives teaches them as short, age-friendly stories covering the Prophets, the Quran’s stories and Islamic history, with a quick question after each one to help it stick.

What age is it for?

The stories use simple, calm language that works from early primary school upward, and younger children enjoy them read aloud with a parent. Archives isn’t intended for under-13s to sign up on their own, so for younger children set the account up yourself and let them learn on it.

Are these online Islamic classes live, or can my child go at their own pace?

Entirely self-paced. There is no timetable, no live class to join and no other students waiting. Your child opens the app whenever it suits your family, does one story and one question, and stops. That is the whole session.

Can it replace weekend Islamic school or a maktab?

No. It sits alongside them rather than replacing them. It builds knowledge and curiosity about Islamic history between classes, but it doesn’t teach Quran recitation, tajweed, Arabic or fiqh, so it works best with a qualified teacher. If you can only manage one, choose the teacher.

Do kids need to read Arabic to use it?

No. Everything is in clear English, so children can learn the stories and history of Islam even before they can read Arabic.

Is the content suitable for children?

Every story is built from historical accounts and written in calm, plain language. Hardship and battles aren’t skipped, because they are part of the history, but they are told without graphic detail. There is no infinite feed and nothing for a child to buy mid-lesson. The session ends when the question is answered.

What does it cost?

You can start for free. A subscription unlocks the full library and extra learning tools when your family is ready for more. Our free Islamic studies page breaks down what each one includes.
