What these classes cover, and what they don’t

Plenty of free Islamic courses online teach fiqh, aqeedah, hadith sciences, tajweed or Arabic grammar, and many issue a certificate at the end. Archives does none of those things. This is Islamic history: the Prophets, the Seerah, the caliphates, the Golden Age, Al-Andalus, West Africa and the Ottomans. What happened, and why it mattered. There is no certificate and no exam.

That trade is deliberate. Most people were never taught this history, and it is what makes the rest of it make sense. If you need Arabic, fiqh or a credential, study those with a qualified teacher and use Archives for the context around them.

Frequently asked questions

Can I really start studying for free?

Yes. You can start learning at no cost, and no card is needed. A subscription unlocks the full library and extra learning tools when you’re ready for more.

Do I need to create an account?

Yes, and it takes about a minute. The account is what keeps your streak, your XP and your place in the story safe across your phone and the web, so you can pick up exactly where you stopped.

Where do I begin if I know nothing?

At the beginning, which is the point. Archives opens with the earliest stories and moves forward era by era, so you’re never dropped into the middle of something that assumes you already know the context.

Is there a certificate at the end?

No. Archives isn’t an accredited course and doesn’t issue certificates or diplomas, so if you need a credential, look at an accredited institution. What you get here is the history, and enough recall to still have it a month later.

Do these courses cover fiqh, Arabic or tajweed?

No. Archives teaches Islamic history: the Prophets, the Seerah, the caliphates, the Golden Age, Al-Andalus, West Africa and the Ottomans. For fiqh, aqeedah, Quran recitation or Arabic grammar you want a qualified teacher or a dedicated institute. Archives is the context that sits around that study.

Is the content accurate and sourced?

Yes. Every story is built from historical accounts rather than myth, and where scholars disagree the story says so instead of picking the most dramatic version.

Can I study Islamic history on my phone?

Yes. Archives works in your browser and as an app on iOS and Android, so you can learn anywhere in about five minutes a day.

Do I need any background knowledge?

None at all. Archives starts at the very beginning and builds forward one step at a time, so beginners and returning learners both start comfortably.
